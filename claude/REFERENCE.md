# Reference — distortions, emotions, techniques

Companion data for `VOICE-SESSION.md`. Definitions are paraphrased from the
cognitive-distortion model in Dr David Burns' books (*Feeling Good*, *Feeling
Great*); the negative/positive mirror-image framing is his.

## The 10 cognitive distortions — both faces

Every distortion has a **negative face** (fuels depression and anxiety) and a
**positive face** (fuels temptations, habits and addictions). The JSON code is
always the one in brackets, regardless of face.

| Code | Name (negative face) | Negative face | Positive face (habits) |
|------|---------------------|---------------|------------------------|
| AON | All-or-Nothing Thinking | Absolute black-and-white categories; short of perfect = total failure. | Everything left to do fuses into one overwhelming blob; the habit feels like the only relief. |
| OG | Overgeneralization | One bad event = never-ending pattern of defeat ("always", "never"). | One slip = the whole effort is ruined, so give up completely ("the diet's blown anyway"). |
| MF | Mental Filter | Dwell on one negative, filter out all positives. | Dwell on how good it will feel, filter out the price afterwards. |
| DP | Discounting the Positive *(positive face: Discounting the Negative, shown as DN)* | Positives don't count ("anyone could have"). | Consequences won't count this time ("I'll just have one" — you never stop at one). |
| MR | Mind Reading | Assume others react negatively to you, without evidence. | Assume others don't struggle like you, or nobody will notice or mind. |
| FT | Fortune Telling | Predict bad outcomes as fact. | Predict the reward as fact ("a couple of beers will fix this; I'll start tomorrow"). |
| MAG | Magnification / Minimization | Blow up negatives, shrink positives. | Blow up how wonderful it will be, shrink the costs ("it'll taste SO good"). |
| ER | Emotional Reasoning | "I feel like an idiot, so I am one." | "I feel like it, so I should." / "I'll start when I'm in the mood." |
| SH | Should Statements | Shoulds, musts, oughts breed guilt and pressure. | "I've earned it, I deserve this" — then post-slip shoulds create guilt that fuels the next round. |
| LAB | Labeling | "I'm a loser" instead of "I made a mistake". | "I'm just a procrastinator" (an excuse) or "I'm such a pig" (a punishment so harsh you indulge again). |
| SB | Self-Blame | Blame yourself for what wasn't all yours. | Beat yourself up for giving in — the guilt-then-indulge cycle. |
| OB | Other-Blame | Blame others, ignore your part. | "It's unfair — others can, why can't I?" Genes/spouse/job as licence. |

Notes for coaching by voice:
- Most thoughts contain **several** distortions. Two or three confirmed beats
  an exhaustive audit.
- In habit work, ER + SH + FT is the classic trio ("I feel like it, I deserve
  it, it'll be great").
- MAG on the paper checklist covers both magnification and minimization; MR
  and FT are the two halves of "Jumping to Conclusions".

## Emotion clusters (Daily Mood Log)

Offer these as families; the user picks the word that fits, or their own.

- Sad — blue, depressed, down, unhappy
- Anxious — worried, panicky, nervous, frightened
- Guilty — remorseful, bad, ashamed
- Inferior — worthless, inadequate, defective, incompetent
- Lonely — unloved, unwanted, rejected, alone, abandoned
- Embarrassed — foolish, humiliated, self-conscious
- Hopeless — discouraged, pessimistic, despairing
- Frustrated — stuck, thwarted, defeated
- Angry — mad, resentful, annoyed, irritated, upset, furious
- Other — the user's own word

Each is rated 0–100 **before** and, after the work, **after**.

## The two conditions for a positive thought

A positive thought only works if it is:
1. **100% true** (necessary condition) — rationalizations and pep-talk never
   help; sharpen until the user can honestly rate its truth near 100%.
2. **Belief-reducing** (sufficient condition) — it must substantially pull
   down belief in the negative thought. If it doesn't, try another technique
   or another angle.

## Techniques to offer while coaching

One at a time, matched to the thought. All are askable by voice.

- **Examine the evidence** — facts for and against the thought.
- **Double standard** — "What would you say to a dear friend in this spot?"
- **Experimental technique** — how could we test this prediction?
- **Survey technique** — would others actually think what you assume?
- **Reattribution** — list *all* the causes, not just "me".
- **Semantic method** — replace the whip: "it would be preferable" for "I should".
- **Externalization of voices** — Claude speaks the negative thought in second
  person; user answers in first person. Swap until the answer lands.
- **Acceptance paradox** — find calm in accepting the kernel of truth with
  self-respect, instead of fighting it.
- **Cost-benefit analysis** — what does believing this thought (or keeping
  this habit) cost and give?
- **Devil's Advocate** (habits only) — see Protocol B in `VOICE-SESSION.md`.

## The TEAM model in one breath

- **T — Testing:** measure before and after; the drop is the outcome.
- **E — Empathy:** listen and reflect first; check "did I get it right?".
- **A — Assessment of resistance:** honor the good reasons behind the problem
  (Positive Reframing) before trying to change anything.
- **M — Methods:** only then, techniques.
